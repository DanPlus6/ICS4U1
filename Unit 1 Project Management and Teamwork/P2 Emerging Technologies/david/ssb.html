Solid State Batteries

What are Solid State Batteries?

Solid State Batteries (SSBs) are a new advanced energy storage technology that can succeed traditional lithium-ion batteries (LIBs). By replacing flammable liquid/gel electrolytes with solid materials including ceramics, polymers, and sulfides, SSBs are safer, more thermally stable, and can store more energy than LIBs. Additionally, they mitigate risks of high flammability, dendrite formation, electrolytic decomposition at high voltages, and leaks that liquid-electrolyte-based batteries are susceptible to.

What are the impacts of SBBs?

Pros
    Impact on Global Cultures
  • SSBs can support development for smaller, lighter, and longer-lasting batteries. Their higher energy density and compact design benefits electric vehicles, wearable electronics, medical devices, and aerospace applications; this leads to greater global access to technologies that depend on portable energy storage and could make electric transportation more practical in different parts of the world.
    • Economic Impacts
  • The development of solid-state batteries is creating increased research, investment, patents, and commercial activity. Patents and research regarding SSBs have grown significantly, led by demand for EVs and renewable-energy storage; Canada has even received over $40 billion in automotive and battery-supply-chain investments since 2020.
Cons
    Impact on Canadian Society & Minorities
  • The raw material extraction, processing and complex manufacturing processes of SBBs can lead to significant natural resource & energy consumption. High energy consumption would lead to increased carbon emissions, and mining and refining activities can damage the environment and also lead to exploitation of Canadian indigenous and minorities’ land.
